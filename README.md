# MSGJO-code
In academic research, the decision to open-source code is often subject to institutional policies and funding regulations. For the code associated with the paper "Multi-Strategy Golden Jackal Optimization for Engineering Design" by Professor Yang, while open-sourcing can facilitate academic exchange and improve research reproducibility, the university’s research office mandates that research code cannot be released before the corresponding paper is officially published. This policy is based on the following key considerations:

Academic Integrity and Research Protection
Before formal publication, the research findings have not yet undergone full peer review or journal approval. Premature open-sourcing could lead to unauthorized use, modification, or misinterpretation of the research, potentially compromising its originality and academic credibility.

Intellectual Property and Priority Rights
Since the study may involve novel algorithms or methodologies, early code release could impact the authors' priority in the field or even lead to intellectual property disputes. Delaying the release ensures that Professor Yang and the research team receive proper recognition for their contributions.

Review and Publication Process Requirements
Many academic journals and conferences enforce strict reproducibility standards, but the peer review process may require modifications or refinements to experiments. If the code is released before final acceptance, discrepancies between pre-publication and published versions could arise, affecting research reliability. Additionally, some journals restrict pre-publication code sharing to preserve review objectivity.

Institutional and Funding Regulations
Universities and research institutions often impose policies governing the management of research data and code, especially in funded projects or collaborative studies. Adhering to these regulations is essential for maintaining both academic credibility and institutional compliance.

For these reasons, the code for "Multi-Strategy Golden Jackal Optimization for Engineering Design" by Professor Yang will be made publicly available after the paper is officially published. At that time, the full experimental code and documentation will be released to support further academic research and applications. Until then, we appreciate your understanding and support in maintaining research rigor and adherence to institutional policies.
